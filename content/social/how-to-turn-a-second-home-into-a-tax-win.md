## LinkedIn

14 days. That's the line between a tax-free rental week at the cabin and a Schedule E filing.

Rent your second home 14 days or fewer a year and the income isn't reportable at all, no matter how much you collect. Cross that line and the IRS starts splitting your deductions based on personal-use days versus rental days.

Take a Hayward, Wisconsin cabin: a client renting it 40 days and using it 20 days themselves lands in the limited-deduction bracket, expenses can offset rental income but no loss carries to their other income. Cut personal use to 12 days and rent it 60, and the same property opens up full depreciation and a potential $25,000 loss deduction, subject to the $100,000-$150,000 AGI phase-out.

Twelve days of scheduling changes the entire tax outcome. If you own a lake place or a condo and haven't run this math, it's worth doing before next summer's calendar fills up.
