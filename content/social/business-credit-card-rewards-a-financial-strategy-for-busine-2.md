## LinkedIn

A dentist in Hudson, Wisconsin puts $18,000 a month through supplies, lab work, and equipment leases. An attorney in Stillwater runs $4,000 through filing fees and software subscriptions. Both ask us the same thing every year: is a rewards card worth it, or just a fee generator with better marketing?

Here's the detail most business owners miss: rewards earned on normal purchases are generally treated as a rebate against expenses, not taxable income. Spend $50,000 and earn $750 back, and the IRS views that as a lower expense, not extra income. But most rotating-category cards cap bonus spending at $25,000 to $50,000 per quarter before dropping to a base rate, so the card only pays off if it matches how your business actually spends.

For dentists, attorneys, and service-based businesses running six figures a month through one card, that mismatch shows up in your margins before it shows up anywhere else.
