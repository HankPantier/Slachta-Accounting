## LinkedIn

A dentist's golden retriever greeting patients in the waiting room isn't a business deduction, no matter how much patients love him. That's the real question a Stillwater client asked us last January, half-joking, about his own dog. The IRS answer under Publication 535: a dog's costs count as a business expense only when it performs a documented function, like guarding a warehouse or working a breeding operation with a real profit motive and recordkeeping. Service animals fall under a different rule entirely, Publication 502, and only help once itemized medical expenses clear 7.5% of AGI. Therapy dogs and emotional support animals, without trained tasks tied to a diagnosed disability, don't qualify under either rule. For dentists, insurance agencies, and service-based business owners whose dogs are genuinely part of daily operations, this is worth reviewing before tax season, not after an audit letter shows up.
