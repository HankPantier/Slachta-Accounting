## LinkedIn

A $9,000 ER visit paid straight from a savings account costs $9,000, full price, no tax benefit recovered. Paid through an HSA, that same $9,000 was already deducted from taxable income going in and comes out tax-free for qualified care. For dentists running a solo practice or small business owners on a family health plan, that's the difference between two identical bills and two very different outcomes on your return. There's a second lever too: if you itemize, medical expenses above 7.5% of AGI are deductible, and a household at $120,000 in AGI clears that bar once qualified costs pass $9,000, which a surgery or a long hospital stay hits fast. An emergency fund buys time. It doesn't reduce what you owe. Here's how HSAs, FSAs, and the itemized deduction do: https://www.slachtacpa.com/resources/why-tax-advantaged-planning-is-the-real-defense-against-medi
