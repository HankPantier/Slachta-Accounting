## LinkedIn

A dentist was paying $380 a month for disability coverage sized to a $220,000 income from 2015. Current income: $310,000. That gap is the real problem, not the premium.

Insurance coverage isn't a checklist of policies to buy. The right amount is the difference between your net worth (or your practice's cash reserves) and the realistic dollar loss a specific risk could cause, life, disability, liability, property. Skip that calculation and you end up over-insured in places that don't matter and exposed in the ones that do.

We see this constantly with dentists, attorneys, architects, and service-business owners across Minnesota and Wisconsin: strong E&O coverage, no plan for what happens to cash flow if a key producer leaves with half the book.

Run the numbers before your next renewal. Read the full breakdown: https://www.slachtacpa.com/resources/insuring-where-it-counts-avoid-over-insuring-in-the-wrong-pl
