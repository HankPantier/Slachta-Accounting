## Payroll that keeps your team paid accurately and on time

Running payroll is one of those tasks that feels simple until it isn't. A missed tax deposit, a late filing, an employee whose direct deposit didn't land, and suddenly an afternoon is gone chasing down a fix. Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services has handled payroll for business owners since 2005, and we built our process around one goal: you should never have to think about it.

We process payroll on your schedule, file the taxes on time, and keep the paperwork straight so you're not scrambling at year-end. Whether you have three employees or thirty, across one location or several, payroll runs in the background while you run the business. If you're switching providers or setting up payroll for the first time, we handle the transition so nothing falls through the cracks.

## What our payroll services include

Payroll is more than cutting checks. Our service covers the full cycle, so nothing gets handled twice or missed entirely:

- Payroll processing on a weekly, biweekly, or monthly schedule, matched to how your business operates
- Federal and state payroll tax filings and deposits, submitted on time, every time
- Direct deposit setup and management for every employee
- Year-end W-2 and 1099 preparation and distribution
- New hire reporting to the appropriate state agencies
- Garnishment and deduction tracking for benefits, retirement contributions, and court-ordered withholdings

If your team includes hourly staff, salaried employees, and contractors under one roof, we handle all three without treating any of them as an afterthought.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: dental-practice-payroll-meeting.jpg | alt: "Dental practice owner reviewing payroll and staffing documents" | query: "dental office owner reviewing paperwork" -->
## Payroll support tailored to your industry

Each of the industries we work with has its own payroll complexity, and a one-size template rarely fits any of them well.

**Dental practices** deal with a mix that trips up generic providers: hygienists on hourly pay, associate dentists on production-based compensation, and front-desk staff with benefits that need to be tracked accurately. Get any of that wrong and it shows up as a cash flow surprise a month later. We've supported dental clients around Stillwater, Minnesota, and across Minnesota and Wisconsin, and we build payroll around how a practice actually pays people.

**Insurance agencies** often run a combination of salaried staff and commission-based producers, where compensation structures shift with book-of-business changes. Keeping payroll accurate alongside those variables requires close attention to deduction and commission tracking.

**Law firms and architecture firms** typically carry a mix of professional staff, support staff, and in some cases contractors or project-based employees. Payroll here has to account for different pay structures under one roof without creating reconciliation headaches at tax time.

**Specialty healthcare practices** face similar complexity to dental offices — licensed clinical staff, front-office employees, and varying benefit elections — with the added layer of compliance considerations that come with healthcare employment. We build payroll around how the practice actually operates, not a generic template.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-left | image: business-owner-financial-review.jpg | alt: "Business owner and accountant reviewing financial reports together at a desk" | query: "business owner accountant reviewing financial reports" -->
## Payroll connected to your bigger financial picture

A lot of payroll vendors stop at the paycheck. That's a problem when your bookkeeping shows one number and your payroll provider shows another, and you're stuck reconciling the difference before a loan application or a tax filing deadline.

Because payroll runs alongside our outsourced accounting, business tax, and advisory services, your labor costs flow directly into the books we're already managing. Cash flow reports reflect actual payroll expense. Budgets and KPIs account for real staffing costs, not estimates. When it's time to file taxes or evaluate a retirement plan option for your team, the payroll numbers already match everything else.

For owners who work with a financial advisor on retirement plans or personal financial planning, that consistency matters. Clean, connected payroll data means fewer surprises and fewer calls asking which number is correct.
