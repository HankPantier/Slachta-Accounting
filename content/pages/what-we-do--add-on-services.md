## Extra support built around your core accounting relationship

Once your books are clean and payroll runs on schedule, most business owners start asking a different set of questions. Should I set up a 401(k) for my team? Why does my cash flow feel tight even in a good month? Add-on services answer those questions without requiring you to hire another firm or explain your business from scratch to someone new.

Think of these as modular extensions to the [Outsourced Accounting](/what-we-do/outsourced-accounting), [Payroll](/services/payroll-services), and [Tax Plus](/what-we-do/tax-plus) work we already do for you. You add what you need, when you need it. A dental practice scaling to a second location has different priorities than a solo attorney trying to smooth out seasonal cash flow, and the mix should reflect that.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: retirement-plan-review.jpg | alt: "Business owner and advisor reviewing retirement plan documents at a desk" | query: "advisor reviewing retirement plan documents" -->
## Retirement plan evaluation and guidance

Most of our business clients, from dental practice owners to insurance agency principals, don't have an in-house HR or finance team to research retirement plan options. That leaves the decision sitting on the owner's desk: SEP IRA, SIMPLE IRA, Solo 401(k), or a full 401(k) with employee matching. Each carries different contribution limits, administrative costs, and tax implications for the owner and the staff.

We walk through the tradeoffs in plain terms, model the tax impact of each option against your current income, and flag compliance requirements you'd otherwise learn about the hard way. If you already sponsor a plan, we review whether it still fits your payroll size and profit picture, or whether you've outgrown it. The goal is a plan you understand well enough to explain to your employees, not just one that checks a box.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-left | image: financial-advisor-coordination.jpg | alt: "CPA on a video call coordinating with a client's financial advisor" | query: "professional coordinating call financial advisor" -->
## Coordinated planning with your financial advisor

You don't need to choose between us and the financial advisor who already manages your investments. Personal financial planning works best when your tax strategy and your investment strategy are pulling in the same direction, and that requires someone on the tax side willing to pick up the phone and coordinate directly.

We join calls with your advisor to align on Roth conversions, distribution timing, entity structure changes, and estimated tax payments before decisions get made, not after. Advisors handle the market side; we handle the tax consequences and cash flow impact. Clients keep the advisor relationship they trust, and gain a CPA firm that's actually in the conversation instead of finding out about a transaction on a 1099 the following February.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: cash-flow-budgeting.jpg | alt: "Small business owner reviewing a cash flow report on a laptop" | query: "small business owner reviewing budget laptop" -->
## Cash flow management and budgeting support

Cash flow is the anxiety that keeps most small business owners up at night, even ones with a profitable P&L. Revenue timing, seasonal swings, and one large expense can make a good month feel like a bad one. This add-on gives you a forward-looking view instead of a rearview mirror.

What this typically includes:

- Rolling 13-week cash flow forecasts tied to your actual bank activity
- Annual budgets built from real historical numbers, not guesswork
- Monthly variance review comparing budget to actual, with plain-language explanations
- Expense tracking that flags creeping costs before they become a pattern
- Scenario modeling for hiring, equipment purchases, or a second location

Dentists and service-based businesses lean on this most heavily around payroll increases, equipment financing, and slow seasons where revenue lags but fixed costs don't.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: kpi-dashboard-review.jpg | alt: "Business owner and accountant reviewing KPI dashboard on a monitor" | query: "business owner reviewing KPI dashboard" -->
## KPI tracking to guide better business decisions

Tax returns tell you what happened twelve months ago. KPIs tell you what's happening right now. For a dental practice, that might be production per hour, collection rate, or new patient count. For a service firm, it's often utilization rate, average project margin, or revenue per employee.

We help identify the three to five numbers that actually predict your business's health, set up a simple reporting rhythm around them, usually monthly, and review them with you so the data leads somewhere. A KPI dashboard nobody looks at is worthless. This add-on pairs naturally with our [advisory services](/what-we-do/advisory-services) work, turning numbers into decisions about staffing, pricing, or capacity instead of just numbers on a screen.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Add on services

**Q: What are add-on services at an accounting firm?**
A: Add-on services are optional extensions to core accounting, payroll, and tax work. At Slachta, they include retirement plan evaluation, coordinated financial advisor planning, cash flow forecasting and budgeting, and KPI tracking, added as a business grows or its needs change.

**Q: Will I lose my current financial advisor if I use this service?**
A: No. Slachta coordinates directly with your existing financial advisor rather than replacing them. We handle the tax and cash flow side of decisions while your advisor continues managing investments, keeping both perspectives aligned before major moves happen.

**Q: Which retirement plan is right for my small business?**
A: It depends on your income, payroll size, and whether you want employees to participate. Options range from a SEP IRA or SIMPLE IRA to a Solo 401(k) or full 401(k) with matching. We model the tax impact of each against your actual numbers before you decide.

**Q: How does cash flow forecasting work for a small business?**
A: We build a rolling 13-week forecast tied to your actual bank activity, paired with an annual budget based on historical numbers. Monthly, we compare budget to actual and explain the variance in plain language so you can adjust before a shortfall happens.

**Q: What KPIs should a dental practice or service business track?**
A: It varies by business. Dental practices often track production per hour, collection rate, and new patient count. Service firms typically watch utilization rate, project margin, and revenue per employee. We help narrow this to three to five numbers worth reviewing monthly.
