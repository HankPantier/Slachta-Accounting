## Indiana's new tip and overtime tax break runs for one year only

Indiana lawmakers passed legislation coupling the state's tax code to the federal no-tax-on-tips and no-tax-on-overtime provisions, but only for the 2026 tax year. Starting January 1, 2026, tipped income and qualifying overtime pay are exempt from Indiana state and county income tax, mirroring the federal deduction created under the 2025 tax law. This is not a permanent rewrite of Indiana's tax code. Unless the General Assembly acts again, the exemption expires at the end of 2026 and tips revert to fully taxable income at the state and county level in 2027.

For employers, that one-year window matters more than the headline. Payroll systems, employee communications, and year-end tax planning all need to reflect a temporary rule, not a permanent one. Business owners who assume this is here to stay risk under-withholding in 2027 or building compensation strategies around a benefit that disappears. If you run a service business with tipped staff in Indiana, Minnesota, or Wisconsin, treat 2026 as a single planning year with its own deadline.

## Who qualifies for the tip and overtime exemption

The exemption follows the federal definition of qualifying tips and overtime, which means it applies primarily to employees who customarily and regularly receive tips as part of their compensation, plus workers who earn federally mandated overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act. Servers, bartenders, salon and spa staff, and other front-line service employees are the clearest fit. Overtime pay tied to hours worked beyond 40 in a week also qualifies, subject to the same federal limits and caps that apply to the deduction.

Business owners themselves generally don't qualify for the exemption on their own draw or salary, since owner compensation isn't structured as tip income. The benefit sits with employees, but it directly affects owners who process payroll, set tip policies, or manage staffing costs in tip-heavy roles. If you employ anyone who receives tips in Indiana, you need to know which wages qualify before you run your first 2026 payroll.

## What this means for service-based businesses and dental practices

Service-based businesses with tipped staff, salons, restaurants, personal services, and similar operations, see the most direct effect. Employees who receive gratuities on top of hourly or service-based pay will see less state and county tax withheld from that tip income in 2026, which changes take-home pay conversations and W-2 reporting for the year.

Dental practices sit in a gray zone worth clarifying rather than assuming. Front-desk tips and gratuities aren't standard in most Indiana dental practices, and referral bonuses or patient gifts to staff typically don't meet the federal definition of qualifying tip income. If a practice does have a tipping arrangement for any role, such as spa-adjacent cosmetic services offered alongside dental care, that income needs the same review as a restaurant or salon would apply. The safer approach for any practice owner is to have payroll and tax staff confirm, role by role, whether the exemption actually applies before assuming it does or doesn't.

## Payroll and reporting changes employers need to make

Indiana's 2026-only exemption isn't automatic inside most payroll systems. Employers need to confirm their software or provider has updated state and county withholding tables to reflect the exclusion, and that the change is scoped correctly to expire after 2026 unless the legislature extends it.

Before running payroll in 2026, work through these updates:

- Confirm your payroll provider or software has applied the Indiana-specific 2026 withholding change, separate from the federal update
- Reclassify and tag tip income and qualifying overtime separately in your payroll system for accurate year-end reporting
- Update employee handbooks or tip policy documents to note the exemption is temporary and tied to the 2026 tax year
- Review W-2 reporting fields to make sure exempt tip and overtime amounts are captured correctly for state filings
- Communicate the change to employees directly so they understand the increase in take-home pay and its 2026 end date

Getting these steps wrong doesn't just create a compliance headache. It creates a mismatch between what employees expect on their pay stub and what actually shows up, which lands on the owner to explain. ## Common mistakes to avoid with the 2026 tip exemption

Most of the risk here comes from treating a one-year rule like a permanent one. Watch for these specific errors:

- Assuming the exemption continues into 2027 without confirming whether the Indiana General Assembly extends it
- Applying the exemption to non-qualifying income, such as owner draws or bonuses that aren't structured as customary tips
- Failing to update payroll software before the first 2026 pay run, resulting in incorrect withholding
- Skipping employee communication, which leads to confused questions about pay stubs mid-year
- Overlooking county income tax coordination, since Indiana's counties layer their own tax on top of state income tax

Each of these is fixable with a payroll and tax review before January 2026 payroll runs start, not after.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Indiana adopts no tax on tips overtime only for 2026 tax year

**Q: Does Indiana's no tax on tips law apply after 2026?**
A: No. The exemption is written into Indiana law only for the 2026 tax year. Unless the General Assembly passes new legislation extending it, tip income and qualifying overtime return to full state and county taxation starting in 2027.

**Q: Do dental practices need to apply the tip tax exemption?**
A: Most dental practices don't have qualifying tip income, since patient gratuities and referral gifts typically don't meet the federal definition of customary tips. Practices with any tipping arrangement, such as cosmetic add-on services, should confirm eligibility role by role.

**Q: Who qualifies for Indiana's 2026 tip and overtime exemption?**
A: Employees who customarily receive tips, such as servers, bartenders, and salon staff, along with workers earning FLSA-qualifying overtime, are eligible. Business owners generally don't qualify on their own compensation, since owner draws aren't structured as tip income.

**Q: What payroll changes does Indiana's tip exemption require?**
A: Employers need to confirm their payroll provider has updated 2026 state and county withholding tables, tag tip and overtime income separately, update W-2 reporting fields, and communicate the temporary nature of the change to employees before the first 2026 payroll run.

**Q: Is Indiana's tip tax exemption the same as the federal rule?**
A: Indiana coupled its tax code to the federal no-tax-on-tips and no-tax-on-overtime provisions, so the definitions largely match. The difference is scope and duration: Indiana's version affects state and county income tax only, and expires after the 2026 tax year.
