## Accounting support designed around how dental practices operate

Dental practices run on a different clock than most small businesses. Patient scheduling drives revenue, insurance reimbursements lag behind the work performed, and payroll has to account for hygienists, associates, and front-desk staff who don't all get paid the same way. Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services has worked with dentists and other specialty healthcare clients since founding the firm in Stillwater, Minnesota, in 2005. We serve practice owners across Minnesota and Wisconsin, plus dental clients nationwide who want a CPA firm that understands chairside economics as well as balance sheets. Outsourced accounting, payroll, and business tax services here are built around the rhythms of a dental office, not retrofitted from a generic small-business template.

## Bookkeeping and payroll built for dental office cash flow

Dental offices deal with cash flow patterns that confuse standard bookkeeping software. Insurance payments arrive weeks after treatment, patient financing plans stretch revenue over months, and supply costs spike around equipment purchases or lab fees. Our bookkeeping work tracks production against collections separately, so a practice owner can see whether a slow month is a scheduling problem or a collections problem before it turns into a cash crunch.

Payroll adds its own layer of complexity: hygienists paid hourly, associates on a percentage of production, office managers on salary, and sometimes a part-time hire covering a leave. We process dental office payroll on a schedule that matches how staff actually get paid, handle the tax deposits and filings behind it, and flag when overtime or benefits costs start creeping up. Expense tracking gets tied to categories that matter for a dental practice, like lab fees, supplies, equipment, and staff costs, rather than generic line items that make year-end review harder than it needs to be.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-left | image: dentist-tax-planning-meeting.jpg | alt: "Dentist reviewing tax documents with accountant in office" | query: "dentist meeting with accountant" -->
## Tax planning and preparation for dentists and practice owners

Most dentists find out what they owe the IRS after the year is already closed. That's too late to do much about it. Tax planning for a dental practice needs to happen throughout the year, tied to production trends, equipment purchases, and entity structure decisions that affect both the practice and the owner's personal return.

We prepare business tax returns for dental practices structured as S corporations, partnerships, and professional corporations, and coordinate that work with the owner's individual tax return, since practice income, retirement contributions, and personal deductions rarely sit in separate silos for a dentist. Depreciation on dental equipment, Section 179 elections for new chairs or imaging systems, and entity-level tax elections all get evaluated before December, not during it. Dental practice tax planning done early gives an owner real decisions to make, like timing an equipment purchase or adjusting estimated payments, instead of just a final number in April.

## CFO and controller-level guidance without the in-house cost

Growing dental practices reach a point where the owner needs more than a bookkeeper and a tax preparer. They need someone reading the numbers and telling them what to do next. Most practices aren't large enough to justify a full-time CFO or controller on payroll, and outsourced financial leadership fills that gap.

- KPI tracking for production, collections, overhead percentage, and new patient numbers, reviewed on a schedule the owner actually looks at
- Budgeting built around seasonal patient volume, staff costs, and equipment replacement cycles
- Retirement plan evaluation, including how a 401(k) or profit-sharing plan for staff affects the owner's own retirement savings
- Cash flow forecasting that accounts for insurance reimbursement lag and financing-plan revenue
- Coordination with the practice owner's financial advisor on personal financial planning tied to practice income

## Financial clarity to support practice growth and transitions

Adding an associate, opening a second location, or planning an eventual sale changes the financial picture of a dental practice completely. These decisions deserve numbers the owner can trust, not guesswork. We work through the financial side of those decisions alongside the owner: what an associate's compensation structure does to overhead, what a second location does to cash flow in year one, what a practice is worth before a transition conversation even starts.

This is where a longer relationship pays off. A CPA firm that has tracked a practice's KPIs and cash flow for several years brings context that a one-time valuation or transition advisor can't replicate. Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services has done this work with dental and other specialty healthcare clients since 2005, long enough to see a practice through more than one growth stage.

**Q: What's included in dental office payroll processing?**
A: Payroll processing covers hourly hygienists, associates paid on production percentages, salaried staff, tax deposits, and filings. It's scheduled around how dental offices actually pay their teams, with ongoing checks for overtime and benefits cost creep.

**Q: How is dental practice tax planning different from year-end tax prep?**
A: Tax planning happens throughout the year and looks at equipment purchases, entity structure, and Section 179 elections before December. Year-end prep only reports what already happened, which leaves no room to adjust estimated payments or timing decisions.
