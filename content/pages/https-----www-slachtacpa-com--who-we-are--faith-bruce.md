## Get to know Faith Bruce

Faith Bruce has spent her career in the numbers side of small business, and for more than a decade that's meant sitting across the table from dentists, service business owners, and entrepreneurs who need more than a once-a-year tax filing. She joined Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services in Stillwater, Minnesota, drawn to a firm that treats outsourced accounting as an ongoing relationship rather than a seasonal transaction.

Her clients tend to describe her the same way: direct, prepared, and easy to reach. Faith works primarily with dental practices and service-based businesses across Minnesota and Wisconsin, though she supports clients nationwide through Slachta's remote accounting and payroll systems. She holds accounting credentials specific to small business tax and advisory work, and she stays current on the payroll and compliance rules that change most often for dental offices.

If you're a practice owner who wants a straight answer instead of jargon, Faith is who you'll likely end up talking to.

## Why dentists trust Faith with their practice finances

Dental practices run on a different rhythm than most small businesses: production schedules, hygiene days, insurance reimbursement lags, and equipment financing all hit the books at once. Faith has built her client base around understanding that rhythm, not just the general ledger behind it. She works with dentists on cash flow timing tied to insurance payment cycles, expense management around lab fees and supply costs, and payroll for teams that mix hourly hygienists with salaried associates.

Most dentists come to Faith because their books look fine on paper but their bank account tells a different story. She digs into why: overhead creeping past benchmark percentages, staffing costs outpacing production, or loan payments eating into what should be owner distributions. That's the gap between bookkeeping and accounting, and it's where she spends most of her time.

Dentists working with Faith get a CPA-supported team that reads a practice's numbers the way a hygienist reads an X-ray: knowing exactly what's normal and what needs attention.

## Services Faith provides to dental and service-based clients

### Outsourced accounting
icon: Calculator

Faith manages monthly bookkeeping, reconciliations, and financial reporting for dental practices and service businesses, giving owners clean, current numbers instead of a shoebox of receipts at tax time. Reports are built to show production, overhead, and margin trends month over month.

### Payroll processing
icon: Wallet

She runs payroll for practices with mixed staff structures, including hourly hygienists, salaried associates, and 1099 contractors, and handles the tax deposits and filings that come with it. Multi-state payroll is routine for clients with locations in both Minnesota and Wisconsin.

### Business tax services
icon: FileText

Faith prepares and files business tax returns for S-corps, partnerships, and sole proprietors, and works ahead of deadlines to flag estimated payments and entity structure questions before they become a problem at filing time.

### Retirement and KPI consulting
icon: ChartLine

She helps business owners evaluate retirement plan options for themselves and their staff, and builds simple KPI dashboards, production percentages, overhead ratios, and staffing costs as a share of revenue, so owners can see the health of their practice at a glance.

## Her approach to client relationships

Faith's clients don't get a generic quarterly report and a form letter. She sits down with dentists and business owners, walks through what changed since the last conversation, and asks what's coming up: a new associate hire, an equipment purchase, a lease renewal. When a client already has a financial advisor, she coordinates directly with them on retirement plan decisions and personal financial planning instead of working in a silo.

She treats budgeting and expense management as an ongoing conversation, not an annual event. That means fewer surprises at tax time and fewer moments where an owner is guessing about whether they can afford a new hire or a new piece of equipment. Clients keep coming back because the answers are specific to their practice, not a template pulled from a different industry.
