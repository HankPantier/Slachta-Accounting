Running a clinical practice means managing patient care and payroll, insurance reimbursement timelines, and equipment costs that don't always arrive on a predictable schedule. Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services has worked with specialty healthcare businesses since 2005, when the firm opened its doors in Stillwater, Minnesota. Physicians, specialty clinics, and healthcare service providers across Minnesota and Wisconsin, along with practices nationwide, bring us their books because they need someone who already understands how a medical office actually runs.

We're not generalists guessing at your overhead structure. Practice owners come to us with real questions: how to smooth out cash flow between reimbursement cycles, how to structure staff compensation, how to know if the practice is actually profitable or just busy. Those are the conversations we have every week with clients in this field.

## Common financial challenges facing healthcare practices

Most healthcare practices we work with are dealing with the same handful of issues, just in different proportions. Recognizing them early is the difference between reacting to a cash crunch and planning around one.

- **Unpredictable cash flow.** Insurance reimbursement lag and patient billing cycles mean revenue rarely lines up neatly with expenses.
- **Expense management.** Supply costs, equipment leases, and facility overhead creep upward without a system to track them against revenue.
- **Payroll and staffing complexity.** Clinical staff, front-desk employees, and part-time providers often mean multiple pay structures and benefit tiers to administer correctly.
- **Missing performance benchmarks.** Without practice-specific KPIs, owners often can't tell whether a slow month is a trend or a blip.
- **Retirement and compensation planning.** Owners and physicians frequently postpone retirement plan decisions because they don't have time to evaluate the options properly.

Each of these is manageable on its own. Left unaddressed together, they're what push a busy, well-regarded practice into financial strain despite a full patient schedule.

## Outsourced accounting and payroll for medical offices

Most practice owners didn't go into medicine to manage a general ledger. Our [outsourced accounting](/what-we-do/outsourced-accounting) service takes day-to-day bookkeeping, reconciliations, and financial reporting off the office manager's desk and puts it on ours, so your internal team can stay focused on scheduling, billing, and patients.

Payroll for a medical office is rarely simple. Between clinical staff, hourly front-desk employees, and providers on different compensation arrangements, errors get expensive fast. Our [payroll services](/services/payroll-services) handle the calculations, filings, and deadlines so nothing slips through during a busy quarter.

The result is a practice that gets monthly financials it can actually read, delivered on a schedule, without adding another line item to the front-office workload. That's the point of outsourcing this work: not less oversight, just less friction.

## Tax planning for specialty healthcare businesses

Tax compliance and tax planning are different services, and healthcare practices need both. Filing an accurate return matters, but so does the work that happens months before the deadline: reviewing entity structure, timing equipment purchases, and evaluating owner compensation against practice profitability.

Our [business tax services](/what-we-do/tax-plus) work through those questions with specialty healthcare clients directly. A practice organized as an S-corp has different owner-compensation considerations than one structured as a partnership between physicians. Multi-provider clinics face different questions than a solo specialty practice. We look at how the entity is structured today and whether it still fits the practice's revenue and growth, rather than treating the return as a once-a-year formality.

Proactive planning also means fewer surprises. When a practice knows in October what its tax position looks like, decisions about year-end purchases, bonuses, or retirement contributions get made with information instead of guesswork.

## Retirement plans and financial planning collaboration

Many physicians and practice owners put off retirement plan decisions because evaluating the options, SEP IRAs, 401(k) plans, cash balance plans, takes time they don't have between patients. We help walk through those options against the practice's cash flow and staffing structure, so the plan chosen actually fits the business rather than a generic template.

We also work alongside the financial advisor a practice owner already has. Retirement planning, investment decisions, and personal financial goals belong with a financial advisor; the tax and cash flow implications belong with us. Coordinating the two means fewer conflicting recommendations and a plan that holds up at tax time, not just on paper.
