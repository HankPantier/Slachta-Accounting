## Find the right form quickly

This page holds the paperwork clients ask for most: onboarding documents for new business tax and payroll engagements, portal instructions for sending sensitive financial data, and checklists that keep tax season moving instead of stalling out. Everything below is grouped by task, so a dentist setting up payroll, an attorney starting outsourced bookkeeping, or an individual preparing a return can find the right link without calling the office first. If you don't see what you need, the last section on this page tells you exactly who to contact.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: client-onboarding-documents.jpg | alt: "New business client reviewing onboarding paperwork with accountant" | query: "business owner signing documents accountant" -->
## New client onboarding documents

Starting service with our firm means a short stack of paperwork, not a mountain of it. New business clients moving into outsourced accounting, payroll, or business tax services typically need:

- Engagement letter outlining scope, fees, and responsibilities for your specific service
- New client intake questionnaire covering entity type, prior-year filings, and banking details
- Tax organizer template for gathering income, expense, and deduction records
- Payroll setup checklist, including employee count, pay frequency, and benefits information
- Request form for prior-year tax returns and financial statements

Business owners in dentistry, insurance, law, and architecture work through the same core documents; the intake questions just shift slightly by industry. Once we receive your signed engagement letter, we send the specific forms your service line requires.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: secure-client-portal-upload.jpg | alt: "Dental practice manager uploading financial documents to a secure portal" | query: "person uploading documents laptop office" -->
## Secure document exchange and portal access

Payroll records, patient billing summaries, and bank statements aren't things we ask clients to email. Every business and individual client gets access to a secure client portal for uploading and retrieving files, so W-2s, 1099s, payroll registers, and financial statements move between your office and ours without sitting in an inbox. Dentists and healthcare practices handling patient-adjacent financial data usually ask about this first, and the answer is the same for every client: nothing sensitive travels by email. If you haven't received your portal login, contact our Stillwater office and we'll set it up the same day.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image-left | image: payroll-bookkeeping-resources.jpg | alt: "Small business owner organizing payroll and expense records at a desk" | query: "small business owner reviewing expense receipts" -->
## Payroll and bookkeeping resource links

Cash flow and expense tracking come up in nearly every first meeting with a service-based business owner. These recurring forms keep both under control between check-ins:

- W-4 and W-9 templates for new employees and independent contractors
- Payroll setup and change-of-status forms
- Monthly expense tracking sheet formatted for QuickBooks Online import
- Mileage and reimbursement log template
- Bank reconciliation checklist for month-end close

Attorneys, insurance agencies, and specialty healthcare practices lean on the expense tracker most, since client volume swings make budgeting harder to pin down month to month.

## Tax season checklists and organizers

### Business Tax Prep Checklist
icon: Briefcase

Covers entity-specific documents: prior-year return, profit and loss statement, balance sheet, fixed asset additions, and owner distributions. Built for LLCs, S-corps, and partnerships filing through our business tax service.

### Individual Tax Organizer
icon: FileText

Walks through W-2s, 1099s, mortgage interest statements, and itemized deduction records in the order our preparers review them, cutting down on follow-up questions during filing season.

### Year-Round Document Log
icon: ClipboardCheck

A running tracker for receipts, charitable contributions, and business mileage, so nothing gets reconstructed from memory in March.

## External links and trusted resources

Some questions belong to the IRS or your state's revenue department, not to us. We point clients to these sources directly:

- IRS forms, publications, and refund status tools
- Minnesota Department of Revenue tax forms and filing portal
- Wisconsin Department of Revenue tax forms and filing portal
- Retirement plan resources for comparing SEP-IRA, SIMPLE IRA, and 401(k) structures

If a link raises more questions than it answers, that's normal. Retirement plan structure especially benefits from a conversation between your CPA and your financial advisor, and we're glad to be on that call.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Forms documents and links

**Q: Where can I find Slachta CPA's client forms and documents?**
A: This page organizes forms by task: new client onboarding, secure portal access, payroll and bookkeeping templates, and tax season checklists. If you can't locate a specific document, contact the Stillwater office and staff will send it directly or point you to the right section.

**Q: How do I securely send tax and payroll documents to Slachta CPA?**
A: Business and individual clients use a secure client portal to upload and retrieve sensitive files like W-2s, 1099s, and payroll registers. Nothing sensitive is exchanged by email. New clients receive portal login credentials after signing their engagement letter.

**Q: What forms do I need to start service as a new client?**
A: New clients typically complete an engagement letter, an intake questionnaire, and a tax organizer or payroll setup checklist, depending on the service. The exact forms sent depend on whether you're starting business tax, payroll, or outsourced accounting service.

**Q: Do you have a tax season checklist for individuals?**
A: Yes. The individual tax organizer walks through W-2s, 1099s, mortgage interest statements, and itemized deduction records in the order preparers review them, which reduces back-and-forth questions during filing season.
