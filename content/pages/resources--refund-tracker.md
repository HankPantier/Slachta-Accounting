## Check your refund status quickly

Your refund is on its way to the IRS or your state's revenue department, not to a black box. The federal "Where's My Refund?" tool at IRS.gov updates once a day, usually overnight, and works for e-filed and paper returns alike. Minnesota residents can check status through the Minnesota Department of Revenue's Where's My Refund page, and Wisconsin filers can use the Wisconsin Department of Revenue's Refund 123 tool. Each system pulls directly from the taxing authority, so what you see there is the same information our office would see if we called on your behalf. Bookmark whichever applies to you and check back once a day rather than refreshing every hour. The data doesn't update that often, and repeated checks won't speed anything up.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: refund-tracker-checklist.jpg | alt: "Person checking tax refund status on a laptop with tax documents nearby" | query: "person checking tax refund laptop" -->
## What you need before you check

Before you open any refund tracker, have three pieces of information ready. Both the IRS and state systems ask for the same basics, so gathering them once saves you from re-entering data on multiple sites.

- Social Security number or ITIN for the primary filer listed on the return
- Filing status exactly as reported: single, married filing jointly, head of household, or another status
- The exact refund amount shown on your return, down to the dollar
- Whether you e-filed or mailed a paper return, since that affects which tracker applies
- Your state of residence, if you're checking a Minnesota or Wisconsin refund separately from your federal one

If you filed with our office and don't have a copy of your return handy, call us and we'll pull the numbers for you in minutes.

## Understanding refund timelines

E-filed federal returns with direct deposit typically show a refund within 21 days of acceptance, and the IRS tracker moves through three stages: received, approved, and sent. Paper returns take longer, often six to eight weeks, because someone has to key in the numbers by hand before the return enters the same system. Minnesota and Wisconsin generally track close to the federal timeline for e-filed returns, though both departments run additional fraud screening on certain returns, which can add a few weeks without any error on your part.

Common reasons for a longer wait include a return with the Earned Income Tax Credit or Additional Child Tax Credit, a mismatch between reported income and employer records, an address change since your last filing, or a return flagged for identity verification. None of these automatically mean something is wrong. They mean the system is doing what it's supposed to do before releasing money.

## What to do if your refund is delayed or incorrect

If your tracker hasn't moved in more than 21 days for an e-filed federal return, or eight weeks for a paper one, the next step is a call to the IRS or your state department directly, not a guess. The IRS refund hotline can tell you whether your return is under review or if a notice is coming by mail.

A refund that's smaller than expected is usually an offset. Past-due federal debt, state taxes owed, or child support obligations can all reduce a deposit before it reaches your account, and the Bureau of the Fiscal Service sends a separate notice explaining the amount withheld. If that notice doesn't match what you expected based on the return we prepared, don't sit on hold trying to decode it alone. Send us the notice and we'll tell you within a day or two whether it's routine or worth pushing back on.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Refund tracker

**Q: How often does the IRS refund tracker update?**
A: The IRS 'Where's My Refund?' tool updates once a day, typically overnight. Checking more than once a day won't show new information, since the underlying data only refreshes on that daily cycle regardless of how often you refresh the page.

**Q: What information do I need to check my refund status?**
A: You'll need the Social Security number or ITIN of the primary filer, your exact filing status, and the precise refund amount from your return. State trackers for Minnesota and Wisconsin ask for the same basic information.

**Q: Why is my refund taking longer than 21 days?**
A: Common causes include claiming the Earned Income Tax Credit or Additional Child Tax Credit, a mismatch between reported income and employer records, a recent address change, or an identity verification flag. Paper returns also take longer, often six to eight weeks.

**Q: What if my refund amount is less than expected?**
A: A smaller refund is usually an offset for past-due federal debt, state taxes, or child support obligations. The Bureau of the Fiscal Service sends a separate notice explaining the reduction; send that notice to our office and we'll review it against the return we prepared.
