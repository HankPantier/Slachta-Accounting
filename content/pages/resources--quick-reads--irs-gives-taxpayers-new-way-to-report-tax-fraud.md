## Why the IRS created a new tax fraud reporting option

The IRS recently expanded how taxpayers can report suspected tax fraud, giving business owners a clearer path to flag problems before they turn into full-blown investigations. For dental practices, insurance agencies, law offices, and other service-based businesses across Minnesota and Wisconsin, this update is worth paying attention to. Fraud rarely announces itself. It shows up in a padded expense report, a payroll discrepancy, or a vendor invoice that doesn't quite add up. Knowing when and how to report suspected fraud protects your business and your standing with the IRS if an investigation ever touches your books. We've fielded questions like this since our firm opened in Stillwater, Minnesota, in 2005, and the process is simpler than most owners assume once you know the steps.

## When you should report suspected tax fraud

Not every accounting error is fraud, and treating an honest mistake like a crime can damage relationships you need to keep your practice running. The line usually comes down to intent. Report to the IRS when you notice:

- Employee embezzlement, such as diverted patient payments or falsified reimbursement requests
- Payroll fraud, including ghost employees, inflated hours, or contractors misclassified to dodge payroll taxes
- Vendor fraud, like inflated invoices, kickback arrangements, or payments to companies that don't exist
- Identity theft, where someone files a return or claims a refund using your business's or an employee's information
- Deliberate underreporting of income or overstated deductions meant to lower a tax bill

Simple bookkeeping errors, missed deadlines, or a bounced payroll run don't belong on Form 3949-A. Those get corrected, not reported.

<!-- block: process-steps | variant: vertical -->
## Step by step process for filing Form 3949-A

### Gather your documentation
Collect dates, dollar amounts, names, and any records that support your claim, such as invoices, payroll reports, or bank statements. Specific details make the report far more useful to the IRS than a general suspicion.

### Download Form 3949-A
The form is available on IRS.gov under "Report Suspected Tax Fraud Activity." It's a single page, but every field matters, so read the instructions before filling it out.

### Complete the form
Describe the alleged violation, the person or business involved, and the tax years affected. Attach supporting documents if you have them.

### Decide whether to file anonymously
You can submit the form without your name. Including contact information lets the IRS follow up, but it isn't required.

### Submit to the IRS
Mail the completed form to the address listed in the instructions. There's no online portal or hotline for this particular form, so it has to go by mail.

## What happens after you submit a report

Once the IRS receives Form 3949-A, it reviews the information and decides whether the claim warrants further examination. Not every report leads to an audit or investigation, and the IRS doesn't publish criteria for what triggers one. If you filed with your contact information, don't expect regular updates. The IRS generally keeps investigations confidential, both to protect the integrity of the process and to shield anyone named in the report until facts are established. That confidentiality can feel frustrating if you're the one who flagged the issue, especially when the fraud touched your own business. It's a reasonable trade-off, though: the same privacy protections that limit your visibility into an investigation also protect your business if someone ever reports a claim against you that turns out to be unfounded. Investigations can take months, sometimes longer, depending on complexity.

## Protecting your dental or service business from internal fraud

Most fraud we see in dental practices, insurance agencies, and service businesses doesn't come from outside hackers. It comes from inside the building, often from someone the owner trusts. Payroll fraud is one of the more common patterns: a bookkeeper adjusting hours, a manager keeping a terminated employee on the payroll, or a contractor billing for work that was never done. Cash flow monitoring catches a lot of this before it becomes a five-figure problem. If your monthly numbers drift without an obvious explanation, that's a signal worth chasing, not ignoring.

Basic internal controls make a real difference: separating who approves payroll from who processes it, reconciling vendor payments against actual services rendered, and reviewing expense reports against receipts instead of rubber-stamping them. None of this requires a forensic accountant. It requires someone looking at the numbers regularly, with enough context to know what normal looks like for your practice.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Irs gives taxpayers a new way to report tax fraud

**Q: How do I report tax fraud to the IRS?**
A: You report suspected tax fraud by completing IRS Form 3949-A and mailing it to the address listed in the instructions. Include specific details about the person or business, the type of fraud, and any documentation you have. You can file anonymously, though providing contact information helps if the IRS needs clarification.

**Q: What is the difference between tax fraud and a tax mistake?**
A: Tax fraud involves intentional deception, such as underreporting income or falsifying deductions to lower a tax bill. Honest mistakes, like a missed deadline or a bookkeeping error, don't need to be reported. The distinguishing factor is intent, not accuracy.

**Q: Will the IRS tell me the outcome of my fraud report?**
A: Generally, no. The IRS keeps investigations confidential and does not provide status updates or outcomes to the person who filed the report, even for Form 3949-A submissions. This protects the integrity of the investigation and the privacy of everyone involved.

**Q: Can I report tax fraud anonymously?**
A: Yes. Form 3949-A allows you to submit a report without including your name or contact information. Anonymous reports are accepted, though providing your information gives the IRS a way to follow up if they need more detail about the situation.
