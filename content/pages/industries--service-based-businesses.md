## A CPA firm built for service businesses

Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services has worked with service-based businesses since 2005, when the firm opened its doors in Stillwater, Minnesota. Two decades later, the client list still looks the same in one important way: business owners who sell expertise, not inventory, and need books that keep pace with billable hours, project margins, and client contracts.

Consultants, agencies, contractors, and specialty firms across Minnesota, Wisconsin, and nationwide bring their accounting to Slachta because the firm treats service businesses as a specialty, not a side practice. That distinction matters. A landscaping company and a law firm both need clean books, but the questions behind the numbers differ: utilization rates, project profitability, staffing costs tied to revenue. Slachta's team works inside those questions daily, for [entrepreneurs building their first team](/industries/entrepreneurs-established-small-businesses) and established owners planning an exit.

## Common financial challenges service businesses face

Service businesses rarely fail because of a bad idea. They fail because cash comes in unevenly, expenses creep past budget without anyone noticing, and owners have no dashboard showing which clients or projects actually make money.

The same patterns show up again and again across Minnesota and Wisconsin service firms:

- Cash flow that swings with project timing, retainers, and slow-paying clients
- Expense management that loses track of subscriptions, subcontractors, and reimbursable costs
- Budgets built once a year, if at all, and never revisited
- No KPIs tracking utilization, project margin, or client profitability
- Pricing decisions made on gut feel instead of real numbers

A firm that has seen these patterns in dental practices, insurance agencies, and law offices knows where to look first.

## Outsourced accounting and bookkeeping support

Most service businesses do not need a full-time controller. They need accurate books, reconciled every month, and a clear read on cash flow before a tight month becomes a real problem.

Slachta's [outsourced accounting service](/what-we-do/outsourced-accounting) handles the monthly close, reconciliations, and financial statements that owners use to make real decisions, not just file taxes at year-end. Reports come formatted around what matters for a services model: revenue by client or project, overhead as a percentage of billings, cash runway. Budgeting support turns last year's numbers into next quarter's plan, and cash flow forecasting flags a shortfall before payroll is due, not after.

For an entrepreneur running a twelve-person consulting shop or an agency with twenty years of history, that difference matters. It is the gap between reacting to the bank balance and managing toward a target. Slachta's team functions as the accounting department without the overhead of hiring one.

## Payroll processing for growing teams

Service businesses grow their payroll before they grow anything else. A design firm adds two designers. A specialty healthcare practice adds hourly staff across three locations. A consulting practice brings on 1099 contractors for a single big project.

Slachta [processes payroll](/services/payroll-services) for teams that mix hourly, salaried, and contract workers, along with the tax filings, withholdings, and classification rules each type requires. Pay runs land on time. Quarterly and year-end filings get done without a scramble every January. As a team grows from five people to fifty, payroll stays accurate instead of becoming the thing an owner worries about at eleven o'clock on a Thursday night.

## Business tax planning and preparation

Tax preparation for a service business happens once a year. Tax planning happens all year long, and that is where the real savings show up.

Slachta works with entrepreneurs and established small businesses on entity structure, quarterly estimates, and deduction strategy months before a filing deadline, not the week of it. Contractors weighing S-corp status, consultants deciding how much to reinvest versus distribute, and agencies managing multi-state revenue all need a plan built around their own numbers, not a generic checklist.

Preparation and filing still happen, accurately and on time, but the real value sits earlier: a mid-year call that flags a tax bill before it becomes a surprise, a strategy session ahead of a big equipment purchase or a new hire. [Business tax services](/what-we-do/tax-plus) from Slachta are built to keep owners ahead of the calendar, not behind it.

## Retirement plans and financial guidance for owners

Most service business owners have more of their net worth tied up in the business than in any retirement account. That balance needs attention long before a sale or a retirement date forces the question.

Slachta evaluates retirement plan options, SEP IRAs, SIMPLE IRAs, 401(k)s, against a business's cash flow and staffing structure, and works alongside each owner's financial advisor rather than in place of one. The goal is coordination: the accounting side and the financial planning side pointed at the same set of numbers. Owners get a clearer picture of what the business needs to be worth, and what a retirement plan needs to fund, well before either question becomes urgent.
