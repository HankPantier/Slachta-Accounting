## A Stillwater, Minnesota firm serving clients nationwide since 2005

Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services opened its doors in Stillwater, Minnesota in 2005. Twenty years later, the office is still on the St. Croix River, and the client roster has grown well beyond it. Business owners from across the country now call on the firm, though the heaviest concentration of clients still sits in Minnesota and Wisconsin.

That local grounding shapes how the firm operates. A dentist in Hudson, an insurance agency in Eau Claire, or an architecture firm in the Twin Cities gets direct access to a CPA who knows their numbers, not a call center or a rotating cast of staff. Two decades of local roots, national reach.

## How we think about your practice or business

Tax returns are the smallest part of what a business needs from an accountant. The bigger questions show up during the year: is cash flow tight because of a slow season or a real problem? Is payroll eating too much of revenue? Should a retirement plan get restructured before it costs money?

That work shows up in a handful of recurring services:

- Monthly bookkeeping and [outsourced accounting](/what-we-do/outsourced-accounting), so the numbers stay current instead of three months stale
- [Payroll processing](/services/payroll-services) that keeps compliance off the owner's plate
- Cash flow management and budgeting built around the business's real seasonal patterns
- KPI tracking specific to the client's industry, from dental production per hour to law firm realization rates
- Retirement plan evaluation, done alongside the client's financial advisor rather than in isolation
- Personal financial planning conversations that connect business numbers to the owner's goals

None of this replaces a financial advisor. It sits next to one, feeding numbers that are actually current.

## What sets our team apart

National dental accounting firms build their entire model around one industry. That works fine until a dentist also owns the building, has a spouse running a separate business, or wants advice that doesn't come from a call script. Slachta works across [dentistry](/industries/dentists), [law](/industries/law), [insurance](/industries/insurance), [architecture](/industries/architects), and general small business, which means the same team that understands dental overhead ratios also understands how a law firm's trust accounting works.

[Amy Slachta](/who-we-are/amy-slachta) and the rest of the team keep client rosters small enough that a phone call gets answered by someone who already knows the account. That's the trade a firm this size makes: fewer clients, more attention, and a CPA who remembers the conversation from last quarter without pulling up notes first.

## Our commitment to your long-term financial health

A tax return gets filed once a year. Cash flow problems show up every month, sometimes every week. That gap is where most of the firm's work with dental practices and small businesses actually happens: reviewing expenses before they pile up, flagging a cash crunch three months before it hits, and checking KPIs against last year instead of waiting for a surprise at year end.

Expense management gets harder as a practice or business grows, not easier. More staff, more equipment leases, more vendors to track. The businesses that survive a slow quarter tend to be the ones that already knew it was coming. That's the standard for an ongoing relationship here: fewer surprises, earlier conversations, and numbers that hold up when a bank, buyer, or advisor asks to see them.
