## A founder who understands small business firsthand

Amy Slachta is a CPA and the founder of Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services LLC. She built her practice around small business owners because she is one herself. Payroll runs that have to happen on time no matter what, cash flow that tightens right before a big invoice clears, the decision of whether this is the quarter to hire: Amy has made those calls in her own business, not just reviewed them on someone else's ledger.

That firsthand experience shapes how she works with dentists, service-based business owners, and entrepreneurs across Minnesota and Wisconsin. When a client describes a cash crunch or a hiring decision that's kept them up at night, Amy has usually lived a version of it herself. Clients notice the difference between an accountant who studied small business in a textbook and one who has run one.

## Why Amy started Slachta Accounting in Stillwater

Amy founded the firm in 2005 in Stillwater, Minnesota, with a simple goal: get business owners out of the books and back to running their business. Too many owners she met were spending their evenings reconciling accounts instead of serving customers or growing their team. She started the firm to change that math.

Two decades later, the mission hasn't shifted. Slachta Accounting still operates from Stillwater, now serving clients nationwide with a concentration in Minnesota and Wisconsin. Amy's approach never treats bookkeeping as the finish line. It's the starting point for real conversations about cash flow, staffing, and where the business is headed next. That's the work she set out to do in 2005, and it's still the work that drives the firm today.

## Focus areas that shape Amy's approach

Amy concentrates her practice on three groups: dentists, service-based businesses, and entrepreneurs running established small businesses. Each has its own rhythm of revenue and expense, and Amy's advisory work leans into the specifics rather than generic budgeting advice.

Across all three, a few questions come up again and again, and Amy builds her client conversations around them:

- Is the retirement plan structure still the right fit as the business and its payroll grow?
- How does this business's cash flow behave month to month, and where are the pinch points?
- What does the budget need to say for the next 12 months, not just the next tax season?
- Which KPIs actually predict trouble or growth for this specific business, and which are just noise?
- How does this fit with the plan the client's financial advisor already has in motion?

Amy treats the financial advisor relationship as a collaboration, not a handoff. She'd rather sit in on that conversation than work around it, because retirement decisions, tax strategy, and day-to-day cash management all pull on the same rope.

## Credentials and professional background

Amy Slachta is a licensed Certified Public Accountant and has built her career specifically around small business accounting, tax, and advisory work rather than large-corporate audit or compliance. Since founding Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services LLC in 2005, she has focused her CPA practice on outsourced accounting, business tax services, and advisory work for owner-operated businesses.

Her client base spans dentists, insurance agencies, attorneys, architects, and specialty healthcare services, industries with distinct billing cycles, staffing models, and regulatory considerations. That range gives Amy a working knowledge of how cash flow and tax planning differ across professional practices, not a one-size-fits-all playbook applied to every client regardless of industry.

## What clients can expect when working with Amy

Clients who work with Amy get direct answers, not jargon. She asks about the business before she asks about the numbers, because the numbers only make sense in context: what's the growth plan, what's the staffing pressure, what's keeping the owner up at night this quarter.

She works alongside each client's financial advisor rather than in a separate lane, so retirement plan decisions and tax strategy stay lined up with the bigger financial picture. Budgeting and KPI conversations happen on a schedule, not just when something goes wrong. Amy treats a client's growth goals the way she treats her own business goals: worth checking in on regularly, not just once a year at tax time.
